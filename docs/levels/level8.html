Surprising result: L-Shape achieves the best MAE despite being geometrically complex. Bayesian NAS at skip=4 (only 6 FEM calls out of 21) still achieves 5.61°C — well below the 10°C engineering threshold. This suggests that for non-convex domains with constrained temperature gradients, fewer FEM anchor points can actually improve PINN accuracy by reducing the step-wise propagation of FEM numerical error.

Why this domain is harder than all Level 8 geometries: The multi-layer structure creates material interfaces where thermal conductivity changes discontinuously. The corner towers create high aspect-ratio elements and strong 3D gradients. PINN architectures that perform well on smooth domains may require Fourier feature embeddings or domain-decomposition strategies to handle the interface conditions accurately.
Takeaways

Key Findings — Level 8

  • ✓
    3D skip operator is feasible — L-Shape breaks the 10°C barrier at skip=4 Bayesian NAS on L-Shape domain at skip=4 (only 6 FEM calls!) achieves MAE = 5.61°C — well below the 10°C production threshold. Same architecture at skip=6 also hits 5.60°C. This is the strongest result in the entire thesis: sub-10°C accuracy with 71% FEM step reduction in full 3D.
  • ✓
    NSGA-II achieves MAE = 2.19°C on L-Shape at skip=1 Both NSGA-II and NSGA-III find architectures that achieve 2.19°C on the L-Shape domain with all FEM steps available (skip=1). This is 2.5× better than the rectangular domain baseline (5.41°C), confirming that domain geometry strongly influences PINN accuracy.
  • →
    Cylinder skip=6 just misses the threshold (10.23°C with Bayesian) The cylindrical domain at skip=6 achieves 10.23°C with Bayesian — 0.23°C above the threshold. Extended training (more epochs) would likely push this below 10°C. Cylinder has good radial symmetry which the PINN can exploit, unlike rectangular domains where all three axes must be modelled independently.
  • →
    Rectangular domain is hardest — all skip>1 configurations exceed 10°C Rectangular domain at skip=2 gives 10.31–10.50°C (all 3 optimisers). At skip=4 the error jumps to 21°C. The rectangular geometry has no structural simplification — the PINN must model temperature gradients in all three dimensions without the geometric constraints present in other domains.
  • ✓
    v2 (2000 epochs) consistently outperforms v1 (800 epochs) Extended training improves all configurations. The per-window MAE analysis shows that early time windows (large gradients, t=0–5s) benefit most from additional training — the high-frequency spatial features near the cooled surface need more gradient descent steps to represent accurately.
